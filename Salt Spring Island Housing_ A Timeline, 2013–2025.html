Salt Spring Island's housing story, 2013–2025

Over more than a decade, efforts to allow suites and cottages on the island have stalled again and again, while the cost of living has gone up. Click a category to show or hide its events.

  1. 2013
    ADU bylaws

    A bylaw allows secondary suites in a pilot area. Few owners use it.

    Source 9: Editorial: Cottage industry, Gulf Islands Driftwood, Sept 11, 2019Opinion
  2. 2022April
    ADU bylaws

    The Salt Spring Local Trust Committee gives first reading to Bylaw 530, which would allow suites and cottages, called accessory dwelling units (ADUs), across the island.

    Source 8: Editorial: Even small steps on housing must be taken, Gulf Islands Driftwood, Apr 27, 2022Opinion
    Opposing view, source 11: Viewpoint: Limits to growth ignored by LTC, M. Leichter, May 4, 2022Opinion
  3. 2022Aug–Sept
    Tsawout First Nation · ADU bylaws

    The Tsawout First Nation writes that the bylaw could affect its rights and title. After a crowded public hearing with views on both sides, trustees pause Bylaw 530.

    Source 4: Housing bylaw process paused, Gulf Islands Driftwood, Sept 14, 2022
  4. 2023February
    Affordability & need

    A local realtor calculates that a family needs $141,500 a year to buy the cheapest home for sale.

    Source 2: Guest column: Magnitude of Salt Spring housing crisis and future needs quantified, E. Booth, Feb 8, 2023Opinion
  5. 2024Jan–Feb
    ADU bylaws · Tsawout First Nation

    A replacement, Bylaw 537, gets three readings. Tsawout Chief Abraham Pelkey writes that the nation was not consulted or even told about the meeting.

    Source 5: Trustees halt ADU bylaw plans due to Tsawout Nation concerns, R. Magley, Mar 10, 2024
  6. 2024March
    Tsawout First Nation · ADU bylaws

    Trustees put Bylaw 537 on hold to meet with Tsawout leadership first.

    Source 5: Trustees halt ADU bylaw plans due to Tsawout Nation concerns, R. Magley, Mar 10, 2024
  7. 2024December
    Affordability & need

    Salt Spring's living wage reaches $26.25/hour, driven mostly by rent.

    Source 1: Salt Spring living wage hits $26.25 per hour, R. Magley, Dec 11, 2024
  8. 2025March
    Other actions

    Trustees ask the province to apply its rule that short-term rentals must be in the owner's own home.

    Source 7: Salt Spring opts in to vacation rental principal residency requirement, G. Sjuberg, Mar 26, 2025
  9. 2025May
    Affordability & need

    A report the province requires says Salt Spring needs 821 more homes in 5 years. Trustees say the report underestimates the need.

    Source 3: Trustees criticize housing report, Gulf Islands Driftwood, May 14, 2025
  10. 2025Summer
    Other actions

    Salt Spring Solutions publishes a report card on which of its housing recommendations have moved forward.

    Source 6: Update: Summer 2025, Salt Spring Solutions
  11. 2025October
    New homes

    36 new homes open at 161 Drake Road. 32 of them are supportive homes for people who are homeless or at risk of homelessness.

    Source 10: Canada and BC announce the opening of 36 homes on Salt Spring Island, CMHC, Oct 21, 2025

Where things stand: As of November 2026, we found no report that Bylaw 537 has been adopted. Check the news for updates.
