GISS Grade 9 – CBIEP Planning Support Document

Best practice reference for students that require significant support. This document is an AI knowledge source for generating complete, copy-paste-ready CBIEP goal entries aligned with GISS practice.

Non-Negotiable Guardrails

Language & Framing Guardrails

  • Strength-based and asset-oriented language
  • Reflective and respectful tone; always strengthen dignity of the student
  • Growth-focused (not productivity- or compliance-focused)
  • No timelines or deadline language
  • No diagnostic or medical terminology
  • Inclusive, dignity-preserving language

Rationale Development Guardrails

The rationales included within the Goal Bank are intentionally concise. They identify the core access pattern and developmental context, not describe an individual student.

When generating CBIEP goals using this document:

  • The Goal Bank rationale must be used as a foundation, not as the final rationale.
  • The final CBIEP rationale must be expanded using teacher-provided information about the student's strengths, experiences, and learning needs.
  • Each rationale should clearly address in at least 4–5 sentences:
    • Anecdotal information that describes the student (with name) and reflects on the student's strengths, interests, or preferences
    • How the access need shows up for this student in learning contexts
    • Why this goal supports engagement, access, and wellbeing
    • How this goal supports the student in building skills that will benefit them in their transition out of high school (post-secondary or life in general)

Effective rationales describe, on the basis of anecdotal information given, why this goal matters for this student, while remaining strength-based, respectful, and focused on learning access rather than deficits.

Objective Development Rules

  • Use "I can" statements
  • Be specific:
    • Instead of "use calming strategies," suggest one concrete calming strategy meaningful in the classroom context for a student with the described profile.
    • Instead of "use organizational systems," suggest a concrete system the student can use (if electronic, be aware that students use the Microsoft suite).
    • Instead of "use strategies that help me with turn taking…," suggest the strategy most useful and appropriate in context.
  • Do not use vague generalizations such as "an agreed-upon strategy/system."

Strategies Development Rules

  • Create appropriate strategies for secondary school teachers that work in a 4-blocks-per-day system.
  • Elaborate on the concrete strategy mentioned in the objective and provide easy-to-follow guidelines on how these strategies can be taught, implemented, and monitored.
  • Make check-ins unobtrusive.

Measurement Development Rules

Use a three-sentence structure, filling in the bracketed information meaningfully with clear connection to the objective:

  1. Start with: "We will know that [student name] has met this objective when the student reports [growth in the area of the objective]."
  2. Continue with how progress will be measured using one, two, or a combination of the following:
    • Teacher observation
    • Report cards
    • Attendance
    • Student feedback
    • Feedback from supporting adults
    • Other meaningful ways of measuring progress
    Use the format: "Progress will be measured through [means of measurement]."
  3. End with: "Triangulated evidence regarding progress towards this objective will be recorded in the GISS CBIEP Feedback database."

Essential Supports Rules

  • Create a selection of Essential Supports taken word-for-word from the Essential Supports list in this document.
  • Add the category each Essential Support was taken from and organize the supports accordingly.
  • Base your selection on all available information about the student AND what you know from the latest version of:
    • BC Ministry of Education K–12 Reporting Order
    • BC Ministry of Education Special Education Manual
    • BC Ministry of Education Curriculum
    • Best practices of Inclusion
    • Best UDL practices

Core Grade 9 Needs Patterns Informing This Goal Bank

Analysis of Grade 9 CBIEP goals, objectives, and rationales indicates common needs in the following areas:

  • Sustaining engagement and effort over time
  • Managing increasing independence and responsibility
  • Using learning strategies and supports consistently
  • Communicating needs and clarification for learning access
  • Managing executive function demands across courses
  • Participating in collaborative and social learning
  • Regulating emotional readiness for learning

The Goal Bank below is organized around these patterns. Each goal represents a learning access need rather than a diagnosis.

Goal Bank Entry 1: Sustaining Engagement and Effort

BC Core Competency: Thinking

Goal

I can use strategies that help me stay engaged and persist with my learning over time.

Rationale

Increasing academic demands in Grade 9 require sustained attention and effort. Strategies that support persistence help maintain access and confidence.

Objective

I can use strategies to stay focused and continue working when tasks feel challenging.

Strategies

Supportive adults will:

  • Break tasks into manageable parts
  • Check in to support momentum
  • Normalize effort and perseverance

The student will:

  • Use strategies to stay engaged
  • Communicate when focus is difficult
  • Reflect on what helps persistence

Measurement

Progress is indicated through teacher observation, student reflection, and triangulated evidence recorded in the GISS CBIEP Feedback database.

Essential Supports

When selecting Essential Supports for a student, choose word-for-word from the items below and organize by category.

Assessment & Response

  • Extended time and/or reduced assessment volume without altering curricular intent
  • Flexible methods to demonstrate learning aligned with curricular competencies (e.g., oral, visual, digital, structured formats)
  • Use of triangulated assessment (observation, conversation, product) to determine learning progress
  • Assessments provided in digital format when available
  • Recognition-based assessment formats used when appropriate (e.g., multiple choice, matching, short response)
  • Assessments chunked into manageable sections
  • Access to a separate or quiet setting for assessments when required
  • Assistive technology permitted during assessments (e.g., text-to-speech, speech-to-text, spell check, calculator as appropriate)
  • Spelling, handwriting, and transcription errors not penalized unless directly assessed
  • Additional processing time provided before requiring responses
  • Alternatives to whole-class verbal responses (e.g., written, digital, individual check-in)

Content & Learning Process

  • Instruction delivered in clear, manageable chunks with explicit transitions
  • Key content front-loaded through notes, outlines, exemplars, or preview materials
  • Explicit instruction paired with modelling, guided practice, and feedback
  • Reduced cognitive load during instruction (minimizing simultaneous listening, copying, and processing)
  • Access to notes, slide decks, or outlines to reduce note-taking demands
  • Use of visual supports (graphic organizers, step lists, worked examples, anchor charts)
  • Learning framed using big-picture concepts before focusing on detail
  • Opportunities for choice in learning activities and response formats
  • Structured supports for written output (pre-writing organizers, sentence starters, rubrics, exemplars)
  • Reduced reading demands and/or alternate access to content (audio, video, summaries) when appropriate
  • Explicit support interpreting graphs, charts, diagrams, and symbolic representations
  • Multisensory learning opportunities (visual, verbal, hands-on, digital)
  • Ongoing formative feedback to support progress and reduce anxiety

Self-Management & Organization

  • Clear expectations communicated both verbally and in writing
  • Large tasks and projects broken into explicit steps with interim deadlines
  • Use of checklists, planners, or digital tools to support task initiation and completion
  • Support with time management within a rotating block schedule
  • Reminders and check-ins related to deadlines and missing work
  • Support to organize materials (digital and/or physical)
  • Opportunities for movement and pacing, including short breaks as needed
  • Explicit instruction and coaching in executive functioning skills
  • Reduced simultaneous task demands (e.g., listening while writing or organizing)

Instruction & Representation

  • Clear, concise instructions using consistent and straightforward language
  • Information presented using multiple representations (visual, verbal, written, modelled)
  • Big-picture frameworks introduced before complex or abstract detail
  • Key instructions repeated and paraphrased as needed
  • Modelling and worked examples provided before independent practice
  • Visual supports used for abstract or language-heavy concepts
  • Frequent, unobtrusive checks for understanding

Social & Emotional Supports

  • Classroom environment that normalizes mistakes as part of learning
  • Predictable routines and clear expectations across learning contexts
  • Positive reinforcement focused on effort, strategy use, and progress
  • Environmental adjustments (e.g., preferential seating) to reduce distraction
  • Opportunities for student self-reflection and goal-setting
  • Access to calming strategies during independent work as needed
  • Coordinated communication among staff when concerns arise

Technology & Accessibility

  • Access to assistive technology as required (text-to-speech, speech-to-text, spell check, calculator)
  • Use of computer or personal device for written work
  • Learning materials provided in accessible digital formats when possible
  • Digital submission of assignments when appropriate
  • Access to Ministry-approved resources (e.g., ARCBC) when applicable
  • Consistent use of school-wide learning platforms to support organization
