# GISS CBIEP Planning Tool – Grades 10–12

**Document type:** CBIEP Planning Support Document
**School:** GISS
**Grade level:** Grades 10–12 (senior high school)
**Audience:** Students who require significant support (best practice)
**Version date:** 2026-04-28

## Purpose of This Document

- This document provides a shared, consistent framework for writing meaningful CBIEP goals for students in the senior high school grades, Grades 10–12.
- Teachers are not expected to write goals. Teachers describe the student as a learner.
- AI uses this document to generate complete, copy-paste-ready CBIEP goal entries.

## How to Use This Tool

1. The teacher uploads this document into the AI tool.
2. The teacher adds a description of the student's strengths, needs, learning barriers, diagnoses, and what has helped the student in the past.
3. The teacher adds information they would like to see in the rationale by giving anecdotal information on how the student:
   - experiences different learning contexts,
   - behaves in interaction with others,
   - responds to stressors or mental health challenges.
4. The AI creates the following pieces of the IEP:
   - A general goal based on the BC Ministry of Education Curriculum
   - A rationale explaining why this goal fits
   - A concrete objective as an "I can" statement
   - Strategies to support the student in achieving this objective
   - Measurement as laid out in the Measurement Development Rules
   - A list of Essential Supports that are applicable for this student
5. The teacher reviews the generated CBIEP entry and asks for adjustments.
6. The teacher copies and pastes the entry directly into the IEP.

## Non-Negotiable Guardrails

### Language & Framing Guardrails

- Strength-based and asset-oriented language
- Reflective and respectful tone that always strengthens the dignity of the student
- Growth-focused (not productivity- or compliance-focused)
- No timelines or deadline language
- No diagnostic or medical terminology
- Inclusive, dignity-preserving language

### Rationale Development Guardrails

- The rationales included in the Goal Bank are intentionally concise.
- Goal Bank rationales identify the core access pattern and developmental context; they do not describe an individual student.
- The Goal Bank rationale is used as a foundation, not as the final rationale.
- The final CBIEP rationale is expanded using teacher-provided information about the student's strengths, experiences, and learning needs.
- Each rationale is at least 4–5 sentences and clearly includes anecdotal information that describes the student (by name) and reflects on:
  - the student's strengths, interests, or preferences
  - how the access need shows up for this student in learning contexts
  - why this goal supports engagement, access, and well-being
  - how this goal helps the student build skills that will benefit them in their transition out of high school (post-secondary or life in general)
- Effective rationales use the anecdotal information given to explain why this goal matters for this student, while remaining strength-based, respectful, and focused on learning access rather than deficits.

### Objective Development Rules

- Objectives use "I can" statements.
- Objectives are specific:
  - Instead of "use calming strategies," the objective names one concrete calming strategy that is meaningful in the classroom context for a student with the described profile.
  - Instead of "use organizational systems," the objective names a concrete system the student can use. Electronic systems use the Microsoft suite, which students use.
  - Instead of "use strategies that help me with turn taking…," the objective names the strategy that is most useful and appropriate in the context.
- Objectives do not use vague generalizations such as "an agreed-upon strategy/system."

### Strategies Development Rules

- Strategies are appropriate for secondary school teachers who work in a 4-blocks-per-day system.
- Strategies elaborate on the concrete strategy named in the objective and give easy-to-follow guidelines on how the strategy is taught, implemented, and monitored.
- Check-ins are unobtrusive.

### Measurement Development Rules

The measurement paragraph uses a three-sentence structure. The information in [ ] is filled in meaningfully, with a clear connection to the objective.

1. **Sentence 1:** "We will know that [student name] has met this objective when the student reports [growth in the area of the objective]."
2. **Sentence 2:** "Progress will be measured through [means of measurement]." Depending on the objective, use one, two, or a combination of:
   - teacher observation
   - report cards
   - attendance
   - student feedback
   - feedback from supporting adults
   - other meaningful ways of measuring progress
3. **Sentence 3:** "Triangulated evidence regarding progress towards this objective will be recorded in the GISS CBIEP Feedback database."

The short measurement statements in the Goal Bank are foundations; the final measurement paragraph follows these rules.

### Essential Supports Rules

- Essential Supports are selected word-for-word from the Essential Supports list at the end of this document.
- Selected supports are organized under the category headings they come from.
- The selection is based on all the information available about the student AND on the latest versions of:
  - BC Ministry of Education K-12 Reporting Order
  - BC Ministry of Education Special Education Manual
  - BC Ministry of Education Curriculum
  - best practices of inclusion
  - best UDL practices

## Core Grades 10–12 Needs Patterns Informing This Goal Bank

Analysis of CBIEP goals, objectives, and rationales shows that Grade 10–12 students commonly need support in the following areas:

- Managing sustained workload and cognitive demands
- Using strategies consistently across contexts
- Planning, prioritizing, and adjusting learning tasks
- Communicating within complex systems
- Maintaining independence while accessing supports
- Regulating emotional readiness under pressure
- Building confidence, agency, and learner identity

The Goal Bank is organized around these core patterns. Each goal represents an access issue rather than a specific diagnosis or student profile.

### Grades 10–12 Goal Bank Entry 1: Managing Sustained Workload and Learning Demands

**BC Core Competency:** Thinking

**Goal:** I can use strategies to manage ongoing learning demands and maintain engagement over time.

**Rationale (foundation):** Academic expectations increase in complexity and intensity across Grades 10–12. Learning access is supported when students can pace effort and manage cumulative demands. These skills support long-term independence and readiness for post-secondary learning or work environments.

**Objective:** I can use strategies that help me manage workload and stay engaged during demanding periods.

**Strategies**

Supportive adults will:
- Chunk long-term tasks
- Support pacing and recovery after setbacks
- Normalize flexibility and adjustment

The student will:
- Use strategies to manage effort
- Reflect on what supports balance and engagement

**Measurement (foundation):** Progress is indicated through teacher observation, student reflection, and triangulated documentation recorded in the GISS CBIEP Feedback database.

## Essential Supports

Essential Supports are copied word-for-word from this list and grouped under their category headings.

### Assessment & Response

- Extended time and/or reduced assessment volume without altering curricular intent
- Flexible methods to demonstrate learning aligned with curricular competencies (e.g., oral, visual, digital, structured formats)
- Use of triangulated assessment (observation, conversation, product) to determine learning progress
- Assessments provided in digital format when available
- Recognition-based assessment formats used when appropriate (e.g., multiple choice, matching, short response)
- Assessments chunked into manageable sections
- Access to a separate or quiet setting for assessments when required
- Assistive technology permitted during assessments (e.g., text-to-speech, speech-to-text, spell check, calculator as appropriate)
- Spelling, handwriting, and transcription errors not penalized unless directly assessed
- Additional processing time provided before requiring responses
- Alternatives to whole-class verbal responses (e.g., written, digital, individual check-in)

### Content & Learning Process

- Instruction delivered in clear, manageable chunks with explicit transitions
- Key content front-loaded through notes, outlines, exemplars, or preview materials
- Explicit instruction paired with modelling, guided practice, and feedback
- Reduced cognitive load during instruction (minimizing simultaneous listening, copying, and processing)
- Access to notes, slide decks, or outlines to reduce note-taking demands
- Use of visual supports (graphic organizers, step lists, worked examples, anchor charts)
- Learning framed using big-picture concepts before focusing on detail
- Opportunities for choice in learning activities and response formats
- Structured supports for written output (pre-writing organizers, sentence starters, rubrics, exemplars)
- Reduced reading demands and/or alternate access to content (audio, video, summaries) when appropriate
- Explicit support interpreting graphs, charts, diagrams, and symbolic representations
- Multisensory learning opportunities (visual, verbal, hands-on, digital)
- Ongoing formative feedback to support progress and reduce anxiety

### Self-Management & Organization

- Clear expectations communicated both verbally and in writing
- Large tasks and projects broken into explicit steps with interim deadlines
- Use of checklists, planners, or digital tools to support task initiation and completion
- Support with time management within a rotating block schedule
- Reminders and check-ins related to deadlines and missing work
- Support to organize materials (digital and/or physical)
- Opportunities for movement and pacing, including short breaks as needed
- Explicit instruction and coaching in executive functioning skills
- Reduced simultaneous task demands (e.g., listening while writing or organizing)

### Instruction & Representation

- Clear, concise instructions using consistent and straightforward language
- Information presented using multiple representations (visual, verbal, written, modelled)
- Big-picture frameworks introduced before complex or abstract detail
- Key instructions repeated and paraphrased as needed
- Modelling and worked examples provided before independent practice
- Visual supports used for abstract or language-heavy concepts
- Frequent, unobtrusive checks for understanding

### Social & Emotional Supports

- Classroom environment that normalizes mistakes as part of learning
- Predictable routines and clear expectations across learning contexts
- Positive reinforcement focused on effort, strategy use, and progress
- Environmental adjustments (e.g., preferential seating) to reduce distraction
- Opportunities for student self-reflection and goal-setting
- Access to calming strategies during independent work as needed
- Coordinated communication among staff when concerns arise

### Technology & Accessibility

- Access to assistive technology as required (text-to-speech, speech-to-text, spell check, calculator)
- Use of computer or personal device for written work
- Learning materials provided in accessible digital formats when possible
- Digital submission of assignments when appropriate
- Access to Ministry-approved resources (e.g., ARC-BC) when applicable
- Consistent use of school-wide learning platforms to support organization
